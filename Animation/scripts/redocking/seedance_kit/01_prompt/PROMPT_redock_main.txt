of @Video1 and @Video2 — same spacecraft, same materials, same lighting, same camera

THE HARDWARE — keep these EXACTLY as shown, do not redesign:

• One central chief spacecraft: a squat cylindrical metallic ring, as in @Image3 —
  brushed silver-grey barrel, dark structural bands top and bottom, and eight small
  circular docking ports spaced evenly at 45° around its outer wall, each port a pale
  ring seal on a short collar.
• Scale: each CubeSat is small next to the ring — about a third of the ring's width.

THE ACTION — one continuous shot, no cuts:
1. OPENING (first third): the eight CubeSats hang in black space around the ring at
   varying distances, arriving from different directions, all noses turned toward it.
   They close in very slowly, drifting inward on a steady, patient glide. Occasional
   brief silent puffs of pale cold-gas thrust correct their alignment. The ring sits
   at frame centre, slowly growing.
2. MIDDLE: the nearest CubeSat lines up with an empty port on the ring's outer wall,
   its long axis pointing at the port, and creeps the last stretch — motion so slow it
   is almost still, a few centimetres per second. It makes contact, settles, and locks
   flush against the collar with a tiny rebound. It goes still, now part of the ring.
3. Then the next, and the next: CubeSats settle into ports around the ring one at a
   time, at uneven intervals — two arrive close together, then a pause, then another.
   Each performs the same slow, precise final approach and lock.
4. CLOSING (last third): the final CubeSat eases into the last free port. All eight are

   slowly to hold on the complete assembled spacecraft, sunlight rimming its edge.
